THE DELIVERABLE

The deliverable: the Project Report

  • 01

    The idea

    The problem it solves, the target user, and the minimum moment of value. In 1-2 paragraphs.

  • 02

    The requirements

    3-4 user stories written as "As a [user], I want [functionality], so that [benefit]". Which ones are MVP and which are backlog.

  • 03

    What you built

    A screenshot or URL of the deployed app. Which features you implemented with AI.

  • 04

    The process

    The most interesting prompt you used. What was hard. What you learned.

  • 05

    How far you got

    You don't need to have finished. What matters is explaining what works, what doesn't, and what you'd do differently if you started today.

CHAPTER 01

The Project Report

A document that tells the story: from the idea to the deployed product.

STRUCTURE OF THE REPORT

Structure of the report — 5 sections

  1. 1

    The Idea

    The problem and the user. One clear sentence anyone can understand.

  2. 2

    The Requirements

    3-4 user stories. The MVP you decided to build vs. what you left for the backlog.

  3. 3

    What You Built

    URL + screenshot. Each implemented feature with a one-line description.

  4. 4

    The Process with AI

    The iterative cycle on your real project: an interesting prompt, what went well, what was hard.

  5. 5

    Reflection

    How far you got, what you'd change, and what the natural next step is.

Total: 1-2 pages, PDF. No specific template. Write it with AI if you want — the content matters, not the formatting.

HOW TO WRITE THE REPORT

Empty vs honest

THE WRONG APPROACH

A report empty of content

  • Just pasting code without explaining what it does
  • Presenting something that doesn't work as finished
  • Leaving out the difficulties (they're the most valuable part)
  • Never mentioning what role AI played in each part
  • Hiding the fact that you didn't get to finish
THE RIGHT APPROACH

Documenting the real process

  • Explaining what decisions you made and why
  • Showing a real prompt you used with Claude
  • Being honest about what didn't work as expected
  • Stating explicitly how far you got
  • Putting the MVP in context: what it is and what's left for later
